LEEVLA: Seeing What Matters in Latent Environment Evolution for Vision-Language-Action

2026-07-09 · 原文 · 翻译 · 2607.08182

LEEVLA is a Vision-Language-Action model that performs reasoning directly in latent feature space, addressing the limitation of existing VLAs which treat all visual tokens uniformly and rely on human-selected external conditions (e.g., subgoal images, segmentation, depth), leading to restricted search spaces and fragility in complex dynamic scenarios. The paper proposes a "where-how" training framework: Drift-Guided Dynamic Prioritization (DGDP) automatically identifies task-critical regions to attend to during training, while Structured Feature Flow Generation (SFFG) models how these prioritized features should evolve in latent space via prototype-to-periphery prediction and neighborhood contrastive learning. Extensive experiments on LIBERO and CALVIN benchmarks demonstrate that explicit task-evidence guidance and structured latent reasoning enhance the generalization and long-horizon reasoning capability of VLA models.

Card 03 核心贡献

核心贡献

  • Proposes Drift-Guided Dynamic Prioritization (DGDP), composed of Dynamic Position Prioritization (DPP) and Semantic Drift Guidance (SDG), which automatically discovers salient and instruction-relevant regions by combining temporal dynamism scores with semantic drift alignment to language instructions.
  • Proposes Structured Feature Flow Generation (SFFG) incorporating Prototype-to-Periphery (P2P) prediction and Mutual-Neighborhood Contrastive (MC) loss, which enforces ordered feature flow based on clustering centroids and preserves local semantic topology via reciprocal neighbor contrastive learning.
  • Demonstrates consistent performance gains over prior methods on VLA benchmarks, validating that explicit task-relevance guidance and structured latent reasoning are crucial for scalable vision-language-action models.
Card 04 方法描述

方法描述

  • Drift-Guided Dynamic Prioritization (DGDP): Computes dynamism scores via cosine similarity changes between adjacent timestamps to identify temporally active regions (DPP), then modulates attention weights using instruction-relevant semantic drift (SDG) to focus on regions whose semantics evolve toward task-relevant directions while suppressing static background regions.
  • Structured Feature Flow Generation (SFFG): Performs joint clustering on multi-view visual features and sorts tokens by Euclidean distance to cluster centroid (prototype-to-periphery). Applies a forecasting loss with a global modulation factor $\alpha$ and learned prioritization weights $\beta$. Introduces a mutual-neighborhood contrastive loss using second-order $k$-NN graphs ($K=10, M=5$) and InfoNCE loss to filter asymmetric or spurious links and maintain topological consistency in latent space.
  • The overall training objective combines $L_1$ action regression loss with P2P forecasting loss and MC loss. Notably, DGDP and SFFG are only used during training, incurring no additional inference cost or memory overhead.
Card 05 数据集与资源

数据集与资源

  • Datasets: Open X-Embodiment (large-scale mixture for pretraining LEEVLA-large), LIBERO-90 (for LEEVLA-mini initialization), LIBERO benchmark (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long tasks), CALVIN benchmark (ABC-D tasks).
  • Model Scales: LEEVLA-large (7B parameters, initialized from OpenVLA-7B), LEEVLA-mini (0.5B parameters, initialized from miniVLA). Vision backbones include DinoV2 and SigLIP.
  • Training Resources: Optimized using AdamW on 8× A100 (80GB) GPUs. LEEVLA-large trained for 50k–150k steps (learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$); LEEVLA-mini trained for 20k–50k steps (learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$). Real-world experiments use a Universal Robots UR5 collaborative robotic arm (6 degrees of freedom).
Card 06 评估与结果

评估与结果

  • Benchmarks: LIBERO suite (four task categories: Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), CALVIN (ABC-D tasks), and real-world manipulation tasks (placing an object, pressing a button, closing a drawer).
  • Metrics: Success rate (%), average success rate, and average episode length.
  • Results: LEEVLA-large achieves state-of-the-art performance on CALVIN and LIBERO benchmarks. On LIBERO-Goal, LEEVLA-mini achieves 96.6% success rate, outperforming baselines like Octo, UniACT, Seer, DreamVLA, and FLOWER. Ablation studies show progressive improvements: baseline (94.8%) → +P2P (95.2%) → +MC (95.6%) → +DPP (96.3%) → +SDG (96.6%). Real-world experiments show consistent outperformance over OpenVLA across Place, Press, and Drawer tasks over 20 consecutive trials each.
